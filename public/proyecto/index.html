2 · The problem

Missions where Houston can't step in in time

  • Up to about 22 min one way to Mars (about 44 round trip).
  • Weeks of communication blackouts around solar conjunction.
  • Moon: the stepping stone, seconds of delay but no quick return.

Sources: McBrayer, Chai & Judd, NASA Langley, 2022 (NTRS 20220013418); NASA/TM-20250003885.

3 · The idea

Detect early anxiety, onboard and private

  • Seven signals: HRV and resting heart rate; respiration and skin conductance; sweat cortisol and sleep; cognitive score.
  • Honest self-report: daily check-in (“I'm fine / Unsure / Not okay”) and a private voice log. A mismatch with the sensors counts as a signal.
  • Personal baseline: each signal is compared with the astronaut's own pre-flight baseline; only the adverse direction counts.
4 · How it decides

Neuro-Load Index and traffic light

Index = 100 × min(SD ÷ 3, 1); 1.5 SD = 50; 3 SD = 100.

  • Green: monitor only.
  • Yellow: private countermeasures (signal at 1.5 SD or more for 10 min, heart rate over 100, breathing over 20 per min, or report and sensor mismatch).
  • Red: crew and Houston notified (cognition drop, short sleep with low HRV, 72 h in yellow, or functional loss).

Project thresholds inspired by NASA measures; not NASA limits and not clinically validated.

Project details

Summary

On Mars and lunar missions, Houston cannot intervene in time. This onboard prototype tracks a crew member's physiological signals, cognition and honest private self-reports against a personal baseline, flags early signs of anxiety with a Green/Yellow/Red status, and guides private countermeasures such as paced breathing. Only the global assessment ever leaves the device, never the voice log.

The problem

On long missions to the Moon and especially Mars, one-way communication delay reaches up to about 22 minutes and conjunction blackouts can last weeks. Ground teams cannot provide real-time psychological support, so early signs of anxiety may go unaddressed until they become a crisis.

The idea

An autonomous, onboard software system that detects early anxiety and supports the crew member privately. It compares each signal with the astronaut's own pre-flight baseline and counts only the adverse direction. The signals are heart rate variability, resting heart rate, respiration rate, skin conductance, sweat cortisol, sleep, cognitive score and a daily mood self-report.

Honest reporting

The design rests on the crew member being able to be completely honest. The daily self-report (“I'm fine / Unsure / Not okay”) and the voice log stay on the device. A mismatch between what is reported and what the sensors show counts as a signal, never as a judgment.

Status system

A Neuro-Load Index from 0 to 100 (index = 100 × min(SD/3, 1)) drives a Green/Yellow/Red status.

  • Yellow: an adverse signal of 1.5 SD or more for 10 minutes, heart rate above 100 bpm, respiration above 20 breaths/min, or a mismatch between report and sensors.
  • Red: cognition 2 SD below baseline in two sessions or 3 SD in one; under 5 hours of sleep for 5 nights together with HRV 2 SD down; 72 hours in Yellow without improvement; or a manual functional-loss override.
  • “Worsening” is a private notice to the crew member.

Privacy and escalation

In Green and Yellow, everything stays onboard. On escalation, only the global assessment is sent (level, index, triggering condition, trend). Audio and journal never leave the device, because audio is not the whole context and would create confusion. The system only alerts. It never orders an evacuation or aborts a mission; people decide.

Countermeasures (simulated)

A paced-breathing guide (inhale 4 s, hold 4 s, exhale 6 s), synchronized with a simulated deep-pressure vest (pressure on exhale, release on inhale), and a taVNS concept panel. The simulated signals respond gradually, and a toggle shows what happens when countermeasures are not effective.

Mission link

The Earth Link panel uses NASA data: the Sun–Earth–Mars angle and the communication delay from JPL Horizons (shown with its live or snapshot status), and recent solar events from DONKI as context. It shows when the link is nominal or approaching solar conjunction, which is when autonomy matters most.

What is real and what is simulated

The status engine, the thresholds, the interface and the NASA data panel are real software. The crew biometrics, the cognition scores and the countermeasures are simulated. No hardware was built and nothing was tested on people. This is a prototype and not a medical device.

Next development route (future work, not built)

A taVNS / phototherapy headband with local Edge AI inference on the device. The thresholds would need validation by clinical experts. No clinical efficacy is claimed.

NASA resources

Data used in the app

  • JPL Horizons API (NASA/JPL): ephemeris of Mars as seen from Earth (2026-11-15 to 2029-01-15), for the Sun–Earth–Mars angle and the one-way light delay. ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/api/horizons.api
  • DONKI, Space Weather Database (NASA CCMC): recent solar events, used only as context. api.nasa.gov

Mars ephemeris from JPL Horizons is fetched live through a small stateless proxy; solar-activity context from DONKI uses a dated snapshot as fallback.

References used for design (not data)

  • NASA-STD-3001 Vol. 1, Rev C: crew health standard.
  • OCHMO-TB-016 and OCHMO-TB-041: NASA technical briefs on behavioral health.
  • Roma et al., NTRS 20190000670.
  • HRP Standard Measures and the Cognition Test Battery.
  • NTRS 20220013418.
  • “Mars Communications Disruption and Delay” (NASA).
  • NASA/TM-20250003885.
  • Langley, Table 1 (conjunction durations by angle), and the Moon to Mars white paper (2° conjunction assumption).

The numeric thresholds are the participant's prototype choices, informed by these references. They are not values prescribed by NASA.

AI use disclosure

The idea, the problem definition, the choice of anxiety as the focus, the Green/Yellow/Red logic and the privacy principles were created by the participant. AI tools (Claude by Anthropic, Google AI Studio and Google Antigravity) were used as assistants to help organize NASA references, convert the participant's criteria into numeric thresholds, generate and test the code, and draft English text. The participant reviewed and approved the thresholds and decisions. No AI model runs inside the app, which is a static website with no AI calls. NASA data come from public NASA APIs. No NASA logo or insignia is used.

Prototype. Crew data is simulated. Not a medical device.
